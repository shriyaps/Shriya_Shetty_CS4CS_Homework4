SDLC Methodologies

I will write a paragraph for each methodology of SDLC. There are many of them but I will suggest a few. My suggestions and reasons are as follows:

  1. The Waterfall model is a straightforward & structured approach where everything happens in distinct phases. For example, planning, designing, coding & testing occur one after the other in sequence. It is best suited for projects with clear & fixed requirements only. This model is easy to follow and manage but it is not flexible which makes it difficult to implement changes later on. It is best choice for small well-defined projects.

  2. The Agile model is a flexible approach that focuses on delivering small & usable parts of the project quickly. Teams work in cycles and adapt as requirements change. Its great for projects where customers requirements frequently change and updates are needed often. However, it requires close teamwork and can be challenging if the team isn't well-organized. The Agile model works well for fast-paced & evolving projects.

  3. DevOps is all about collaboration between development & operation teams. It uses automation to speed up building, testing & delivering software. This method ensures faster updates & high-quality releases but it requires strong tools & processes in place. DevOps is perfect for projects that need constant improvements such as modern web apps or cloud services.

Among so many SDLC Methodologies the ones that are in the Agile classification are better. Among Agile SDLC Methods, I have also a few suggestions

  1. Scrum: A framework that divides work into cycles called sprints. It is ideal for projects requiring quick feedback loops & progress.

  2. Lean Development: Focuses on optimizing efficiency by eliminating waste, emphasizing fast delivery and continuously improving the process. It is great for projects with a focus on value-driven outcomes.

  3. Extreme Programming (XP): Focuses on writing simple code, testing it often and working closely with the team and users to make quick improvements.
